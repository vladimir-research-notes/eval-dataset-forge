Milestone 7 — done

Split construction

A split is a modelling decision, not a coin flip. This is the first stage that changes the data rather than describing it — so it is the first whose output is verified rather than asserted.

One algorithm, five strategies

Every strategy deals indivisible units into splits until each split's quota is full, and they differ only in what a unit is. That unit is a Block, and the choice of block is the choice of which dependence between examples the split refuses to cut through.

A cluster sitting entirely inside test leaks nothing across splits but still makes two correlated examples look like two independent ones, which quietly narrows every confidence interval Milestone 9 computes. The result worth reading twice

The grouping key a dataset hands you is not the one that closes the leak.

Every planted duplicate in the fixtures carries a fresh source_id: src-fr-capital → src-fr-capital-dup, src-fizzbuzz → src-fizzbuzz-para. That is exactly how a real corpus records a paraphrase written as a new record. So source-grouped, whose entire purpose is to stop leakage through shared provenance, leaves every one of them free to straddle train and test.

Sharper still: on the three fixtures where no source_id is shared at all, source-grouped produces a bit-for-bit identical assignment to random at every seed. It is not a weaker control there; it is the same function. A test asserts it.

Only blocks derived from example content close the channel. Provenance metadata describes where an example came from; it does not know what the example says.

The second result worth reading twice

Stratifying on more facets makes the test set less representative, not more. Total variation distance between the test split's marginal and the corpus's, mean over 20 seeds, pooled 56-example corpus:

Joint stratification is worse on both facets than the single-facet stratification of either. Strata multiply when facets are combined while the corpus does not: five families × four difficulty levels gives 17 non-empty strata over 56 examples, a mean of 3.3 each. A stratum of three cannot fill a dev split at ratio 0.1 — its whole quota rounds to zero — so every stratum starves the small splits at once.

It also guarantees an empty cell. The corpus's three hard examples sit in three different family strata, each of size one, and a stratum of one apportions entirely to train. No hard example reaches the test set, so the benchmark cannot measure difficulty. Two warnings surface it, and the default is a single facet.

Deeper: apportion first, then deal

Quotas become integers before any block moves, by the largest-remainder rule: floor each quota, then hand the leftovers to the largest fractional parts. Rounding afterwards — greedily chasing a float deficit — starves the small splits, because a stratum of three examples always shows dev a deficit of 0.3 that no assignment can ever close, and the greedy keeps trying: at n=3 and 0.7/0.1/0.2 it lands on 1/1/1. Apportioning first gives 2/0/1, and the unfillable dev quota becomes a warning instead of a silent theft from train.

With singleton blocks the deal reproduces the apportioned targets exactly (asserted over every fixture × 20 seeds). With larger blocks it cannot — three examples do not divide 0.7/0.1/0.2 — so blocks are dealt largest-first, leaving the singletons to absorb the rounding error. The builder never forces a ratio and never drops an example.

Deeper: the label-audit hand-off

Milestone 6 produces blocking_example_ids — examples whose gold answer no automated check can resolve. Scoring a system against a label that needs adjudication measures the label, not the system.

On the pooled corpus an adversarial split without quarantine scores on six of them. --quarantine-blocking moves all fourteen to train. The cost is real and reported rather than hidden: test shrinks from 12 to 8 examples and its achieved ratio falls from 0.214 to 0.143, at which point the builder warns that test can no longer measure the unlabelled difficulty bucket.

Nothing is deleted. Quarantined and duplicate examples move to train; removing an example from a dataset is the release builder's decision (Milestone 10), recorded in a manifest a reader can audit.

The guarantee is inherited from a heuristic. After an adversarial build, zero residual overlap holds by construction — the blocks are the deduplication clusters. So the guarantee is exactly as good as the near-duplicate stage's recall, and no better. ret-001/ret-002 is a planted cross-split paraphrase that no lexical threshold can reach; the adversarial split straddles it exactly as a random split would, and the verifier — which reuses the same ladder — reports a clean zero. A test pins the miss so the heuristic guarantee cannot be quietly upgraded to a promise. Relatedly, retrieval_toy's all-zero row measures the near stage's answer gate rather than the data; pass --ignore-answer-gate for retrieval-style datasets.

CLI

evalforge build-splits --dataset datasets/fixtures/qa_toy/examples.jsonl \
    --strategy adversarial --quarantine-blocking --fail-on-leakage \
    --out results/processed/qa_toy_adversarial

Writes <out>/splits/{train,dev,test}.jsonl (byte-identical across runs) and <out>/split_report.json. --fail-on-leakage exits 1 when the built splits still contain a detectable cross-split duplicate cluster — the check a CI job would gate a release on. It exits 1 for random on qa_toy and 0 for adversarial.

Status

Open questions

  • eval_splits=("test",) — protect only test, allowing duplicates in dev — changes nothing on this corpus. Duplicate blocks are dealt first and train's target dwarfs dev's, so they never reach dev even when allowed to. A synthetic corpus exercises it. Whether the knob earns its surface area is a judgement call.
  • A block spanning strata (qa-003 is easy, qa-012 is medium) is resolved by plurality. The alternative — cutting the block — would reintroduce the leakage the block exists to prevent, but plurality distorts the facet by exactly the minority members.
  • Difficulty stratification reads the declared difficulty_tags, not a modelled score. Milestone 8 calibrates difficulty; whether splits should then stratify on the calibrated bucket — letting a heuristic define the test set it is later evaluated on — is deliberately left open.
