Leakage & contamination

Leakage is any path by which a system scores well without demonstrating the capability the benchmark intends to measure. We check four kinds.

1 · Train/test overlap

Exact or normalized duplicates that straddle two splits. Reuses the dedup machinery but restricts attention to cross-split pairs.

2 · Answer-in-prompt

The expected_output (or a close variant) appears verbatim in the input. Normalized substring / n-gram containment above a threshold; the matched span is reported as evidence.

3 · High n-gram overlap

For each test example, the maximum n-gram overlap (default n=8–13) against a supplied reference corpus (train split or a public-safe reference). A standard decontamination-style signal.

4 · Retrieval contamination

For retrieval-family examples, whether a retrieved document contains a label artifact — the gold answer embedded in a passage, or a document that is the labelled answer — trivializing the retrieval step.

We can only measure overlap against text we are given. Contamination against unseen (e.g. private pretraining) corpora is undetectable here — absence of a flag is not proof of no contamination. n-gram checks false-positive on boilerplate; the matched span is always reported for review.
