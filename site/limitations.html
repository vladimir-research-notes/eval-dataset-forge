Limitations & non-goals

Being explicit about what this tool does not do is part of its value.

Limitations

  • Fixtures are synthetic. They demonstrate that the audits fire; they are not evidence about any real benchmark's quality.
  • Most checks are heuristic. Near-duplicate, answer-leakage, n-gram, difficulty, and ambiguity checks each have documented false-positive/negative modes.
  • Contamination we cannot see. Overlap is measured only against provided reference text; private pretraining contamination is undetectable — absence of a flag is not proof.
  • Statistics quantify sampling noise only, not whether the examples are representative, and use simple percentile/resampling methods.
  • Small datasets. Stratification and ranking stability are inherently limited at small N; the tool reports achieved distributions and instability warnings rather than pretending otherwise.
  • PII/secret detection is pattern- and entropy-based. The validation layer removes only what its regexes and entropy heuristic recognize — it misses novel formats (false negatives) and can flag benign high-entropy or digit-heavy strings (false positives). It is a project-local sanitizer, not a DLP guarantee.
  • Near-duplicate detection is lexical, and measurably incomplete. Dedup recovers 4 of 5 planted paraphrases with TF-IDF (3 of 5 with pure-Python Jaccard). One planted pair is unreachable by any threshold — a genuine non-duplicate in the same fixture scores higher — and same-template questions sharing a gold answer are a standing false positive. Thresholds are tuned in-sample on 56 synthetic examples. Exact and normalized dedup, by contrast, have no false positives.
  • Leakage checks are bounded by the text they are given. Cross-split overlap inherits every near-duplicate blind spot; answer-in-prompt detection is lexical, so it sees a quoted answer and misses an entailed one, and cannot tell a leak from a legitimate restatement; the retrieval artifact marker is a curated list of annotation conventions, not a general detector; and paraphrased duplicate passages are not detected at all. The label-vocabulary guard deliberately trades a false positive for a false negative, and prints what it ignored.
  • The label audit sees structure, never truth. A mislabeled gold answer that is well-formed, unanimous, unambiguous, and correctly shaped is invisible to every check in the stage — and it is the label defect that does the most damage to a benchmark. Three such defects are planted in the fixtures (qa-006, bio-009, saf-006), marked auto_detectable: false, and asserted by a regression test to stay undetected. Catching them needs a second annotator or a model. Ambiguity detection is lexical, so it misses ambiguity that is semantic or over-specified; conflict detection compares values lexically, so two annotators writing "a drug" and "pharmaceutical" are recorded as disagreeing.
  • A split guarantee is only as good as the clusters it is built from. The adversarial strategy drives residual cross-split overlap to zero by construction — its indivisible blocks are the deduplication ladder's clusters — so the guarantee is exactly as strong as the near stage's recall and no stronger. ret-001/ret-002 is a planted cross-split paraphrase no lexical threshold can reach; the adversarial split straddles it exactly as a random split would, and the verifier, reusing the same ladder, reports a clean zero. A test pins the miss. Relatedly, a strategy that closes the channel its metadata describes may close nothing at all: every planted duplicate carries a fresh source_id, so source-grouping leaves all of them free to straddle and, on three of five fixtures, is bit-for-bit identical to a random split.
  • The difficulty score does not measure difficulty. On these fixtures its ranking is not distinguishable from a random ranking of the hand-assigned tags (Spearman ρ = +0.097, permutation p = 0.497, n = 51), and sweeping the whole weight simplex does not rescue it. What it measures is task_family, which explains 43% of the score's variance against 1% of the declared label's; the highest-scoring example in the corpus is a paraphrase of FizzBuzz declared easy. The score is therefore shipped, reported, and explicitly not certified: trustworthy is False, --fail-on-uncalibrated exits 1, and nothing downstream consumes the bucket as a label. Two caveats on the null itself: it is equally consistent with "the heuristic is wrong" and "the tags are noisy" — three hard examples cannot separate them — and detecting an effect of the observed size at 80% power would need roughly 824 labelled examples. The stage also inherits a defect from the label audit: an example with a missing gold answer scores at the floor of the output-complexity signal and so looks easy.

Non-goals

  • Not a certifier of universal benchmark quality.
  • Not a semantic / embedding dedup service (out of scope for the offline build).
  • Not a medical, legal, or security authority.
  • Not a data-labeling platform or annotation UI (possible future direction).
  • Not a replacement for human judgement on ambiguous labels.
Full detail in docs/LIMITATIONS.md and docs/SAFETY.md. Each heuristic's failure modes are also documented in its own methodology doc.
