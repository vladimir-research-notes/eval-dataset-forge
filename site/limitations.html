Limitations & non-goals

Being explicit about what this tool does not do is part of its value.

Limitations

  • Fixtures are synthetic. They demonstrate that the audits fire; they are not evidence about any real benchmark's quality.
  • Most checks are heuristic. Near-duplicate, answer-leakage, n-gram, difficulty, and ambiguity checks each have documented false-positive/negative modes.
  • Contamination we cannot see. Overlap is measured only against provided reference text; private pretraining contamination is undetectable — absence of a flag is not proof.
  • The i.i.d. bootstrap is wrong on a corpus with duplicates, and ours still is — a little. Measured against known ground truth, the textbook percentile interval covers 0.882 at a nominal 0.95 when duplicate clusters are present, so statistics resamples blocks instead (0.937). But it can only correct for dependence the dedup ladder can see: the reported design effect is a lower bound and the intervals remain slightly too narrow.
  • A bootstrap "win fraction" is not a p-value. The widespread "A wins 95% of resamples" rule has a measured type-I error of 11.4% against a nominal 5%. We report it and decide with a Holm-corrected, cluster-aware sign-flip test — which is itself conservative (0.029) on paired binary scores.
  • Statistics quantify sampling noise only, not whether the examples are representative. The demo's system scores are synthetic; the block structure is real, the systems are not, and no claim about any model follows.
  • Small datasets. Stratification and ranking stability are inherently limited at small N; the tool reports achieved distributions and instability warnings rather than pretending otherwise. On the pooled corpus the smallest detectable system gap is 0.321 accuracy points, and deleting one duplicate cluster reverses the demo leaderboard.
  • PII/secret detection is pattern- and entropy-based. The validation layer removes only what its regexes and entropy heuristic recognize — it misses novel formats (false negatives) and can flag benign high-entropy or digit-heavy strings (false positives). It is a project-local sanitizer, not a DLP guarantee.
  • Near-duplicate detection is lexical, and measurably incomplete. Dedup recovers 4 of 5 planted paraphrases with TF-IDF (3 of 5 with pure-Python Jaccard). One planted pair is unreachable by any threshold — a genuine non-duplicate in the same fixture scores higher — and same-template questions sharing a gold answer are a standing false positive. Thresholds are tuned in-sample on 56 synthetic examples. Exact and normalized dedup, by contrast, have no false positives.
  • Leakage checks are bounded by the text they are given. Cross-split overlap inherits every near-duplicate blind spot; answer-in-prompt detection is lexical, so it sees a quoted answer and misses an entailed one, and cannot tell a leak from a legitimate restatement; the retrieval artifact marker is a curated list of annotation conventions, not a general detector; and paraphrased duplicate passages are not detected at all. The label-vocabulary guard deliberately trades a false positive for a false negative, and prints what it ignored.
  • The label audit sees structure, never truth. A mislabeled gold answer that is well-formed, unanimous, unambiguous, and correctly shaped is invisible to every check in the stage — and it is the label defect that does the most damage to a benchmark. Three such defects are planted in the fixtures (qa-006, bio-009, saf-006), marked auto_detectable: false, and asserted by a regression test to stay undetected. Catching them needs a second annotator or a model. Ambiguity detection is lexical, so it misses ambiguity that is semantic or over-specified; conflict detection compares values lexically, so two annotators writing "a drug" and "pharmaceutical" are recorded as disagreeing.
  • A split guarantee is only as good as the clusters it is built from. The adversarial strategy drives residual cross-split overlap to zero by construction — its indivisible blocks are the deduplication ladder's clusters — so the guarantee is exactly as strong as the near stage's recall and no stronger. ret-001/ret-002 is a planted cross-split paraphrase no lexical threshold can reach; the adversarial split straddles it exactly as a random split would, and the verifier, reusing the same ladder, reports a clean zero. A test pins the miss. Relatedly, a strategy that closes the channel its metadata describes may close nothing at all: every planted duplicate carries a fresh source_id, so source-grouping leaves all of them free to straddle and, on three of five fixtures, is bit-for-bit identical to a random split.
  • The difficulty score does not measure difficulty. On these fixtures its ranking is not distinguishable from a random ranking of the hand-assigned tags (Spearman ρ = +0.097, permutation p = 0.497, n = 51), and sweeping the whole weight simplex does not rescue it. What it measures is task_family, which explains 43% of the score's variance against 1% of the declared label's; the highest-scoring example in the corpus is a paraphrase of FizzBuzz declared easy. The score is therefore shipped, reported, and explicitly not certified: trustworthy is False, --fail-on-uncalibrated exits 1, and nothing downstream consumes the bucket as a label. Two caveats on the null itself: it is equally consistent with "the heuristic is wrong" and "the tags are noisy" — three hard examples cannot separate them — and detecting an effect of the observed size at 80% power would need roughly 824 labelled examples. The stage also inherits a defect from the label audit: an example with a missing gold answer scores at the floor of the output-complexity signal and so looks easy.
  • A release manifest detects accident, not an adversary. manifest.json cannot contain its own digest, so anyone able to rewrite the data can rewrite the hashes, the content_id, and the eval_id that describe it — and verify-release will report the result as internally consistent. It is an integrity check, not an authenticity one. Signing is out of scope and out of the dependency budget. A reader who already knows the published content_id can still detect the substitution; test_verify_release_cannot_detect_a_rewritten_manifest asserts the hole rather than leaving it implied.
  • A version bump is a claim about scores, not about validity. The minor bump says a train-only change cannot move a fixed system's test score. It does not say the release is still a valid benchmark: a train-only addition can introduce train→test leakage that changes what that score measures for a system trained on the release. Every minor emits a warning saying so; the delta classifies and does not re-audit. The delta also joins on example_id, so renumbering ids without changing content reads as a full removal plus addition and forces a major — conservative, and noisy. And eval_id is blind to a change of metric: it can say the arrangement moved, not that the scoring function did.
  • The split seed is an experimental parameter, and a large one. On the pooled fixtures, varying only the seed moves the reported headline number with sd 0.130 against a top-two system gap of 0.036 — so the test split reproduces the corpus's own ordering 48.7% of the time under random. Any single reported number from a corpus this size is an estimate with a standard error larger than the effect. Both content_id and eval_id are recorded so a reader can at least tell whether two numbers are answering the same question. Every count a release carries — block count, duplicate clusters, leakage flags — is also a lower bound, inheriting the deduplication stage's lexical recall.
  • A dataset card is a rendering of evidence, not evidence. It is generated from manifest.json and audit.json and recomputes nothing, so its "known issues" cannot drift — a guarantee narrower than it sounds. Its counts inherit every upstream bound, so a heuristic zero is what the check found, not what is there. Four sections (purpose, construction, recommended use, non-goals) are unverified curator claims; the card labels them as such and names any left blank, but nothing checks whether they are true. And because AuditResult records the difficulty distribution but not the calibration verdict, the card carries an unconditional note rather than a warning that fires only when the heuristic failed its permutation test. Milestone 13 shows the same missing field also denies every downstream consumer a verdict: difficulty_is_calibrated is permanently undecidable.
  • An "optional accelerator" changes the answer, not the speed. scikit-learn is documented as an optional accelerator with a pure-Python fallback. It is a different detector: near-duplicate detection resolves to tfidf iff it is importable, the two methods disagree, duplicates become blocks, the adversarial split will not cut through a block, and eval_id folds the split in. Rebuilding bio_toy from identical bytes, an identical command and an identical seed gives the same content_id and a different eval_id without the package. A release records the method that ran; nothing in a release directory can verify it, because verifying it means rebuilding. See Integration contracts.
  • Dedup findings do not compose, so neither do the counts derived from them. TF-IDF's idf is computed over the collection, so a near-duplicate verdict is a property of a pair and its corpus. The five fixtures hold 45 independent blocks apart and 44 pooled at 56 examples either way, with zero cross-fixture duplicate edges; and safety_toy passes a one-cluster near-duplicate budget alone and fails it inside the pooled corpus. Example count is additive; statistical power is not. Under the jaccard fallback both properties hold — composability is a property of the similarity function. Audit the corpus you will score on.
  • audit.json records what was found, never what was looked at. Zero findings over zero retrieved passages and zero findings over a thousand clean passages serialize identically. Milestone 13's gate turns this into a third verdict, undecidable, which never counts as a pass — but the underlying artifact still carries no coverage record.
  • Prose has no way to fail, so it rots. Milestone 13 corrected a sentence it had measured false, in the one file that was generated and test-pinned; the identical sentence survived in three files no test read, including the README. Milestone 14 added an auditor for the documentation. It checks the checkable surface — links, commands, cited counts, cited example ids, retired phrasings, and numbers bound to the command that produces them. It does not check that the code is correct, it matches retired claims as phrasings rather than propositions (a paraphrase evades it), and its recall over "false sentences in this repository" is unmeasurable, because no ground-truth list of false sentences exists.
  • This website is not audited. The documentation checks read Markdown. These pages restate the docs' claims in HTML and nothing verifies that the two agree.

Non-goals

  • Not a certifier of universal benchmark quality.
  • Not a semantic / embedding dedup service (out of scope for the offline build).
  • Not a medical, legal, or security authority.
  • Not a data-labeling platform or annotation UI (possible future direction).
  • Not a replacement for human judgement on ambiguous labels.
Full detail in docs/LIMITATIONS.md and docs/SAFETY.md. Each heuristic's failure modes are also documented in its own methodology doc.
