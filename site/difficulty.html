Difficulty calibration

A benchmark that is all-easy or all-hard stops discriminating between systems. So we estimate a transparent, model-free difficulty proxy per example and report the distribution it induces — and then we check whether the proxy means anything. On these fixtures it does not, and this page explains how we know.

The headline. The heuristic's ranking is not distinguishable from a random ranking of the hand-assigned difficulty_tags: Spearman ρ = +0.097, permutation p = 0.497, n = 51. What it measures instead is task_family — 43% of the score's variance, against 1% of the declared label's. The single highest-scoring example in the corpus is a paraphrase of FizzBuzz, declared easy.

The score

Five surface signals, each compressed to [0, 1] and combined by fixed, documented weights. Each signal states the difficulty hypothesis it encodes, so a reader can judge the hypothesis rather than the arithmetic.

signalweightcapraw quantity
input_length0.2032 tokensword tokens in input
output_complexity0.2032 tokensword tokens in the canonical expected_output
reasoning_steps0.304 cuessequential / enumeration / aggregation cues
evidence_items0.154 itemsretrieval passages + annotated entities
tool_use0.15indicatormetadata declares a tool/lookup requirement

Three properties are load-bearing. The score is exactly the sum of its components, bit-for-bit — nothing is rounded, because rounding to six places breaks the equality and with it the explainability claim. Counts are log-compressed, not min–maxed (log1p(x)/log1p(cap)), so no corpus statistic enters the score and a score means the same thing in every dataset. And no signal reads difficulty_tags.

Why the scorer is blind to the labels

An earlier design listed the ambiguous/hard tags and the hand-assigned fixture difficulty as scoring signals, while also proposing to sanity-check the score against those same labels. That is circular. Both were removed, and the cost of forgetting is measurable — blending the declared bucket back into the score:

A 15% leak turns a heuristic indistinguishable from noise into one that would have been written up as working. A test asserts that rewriting an example's declaration leaves its score bit-identical.

Calibration: a falsifiable question

Every other stage can be scored against defects planted in the fixtures. A difficulty proxy cannot — there is no ground-truth difficulty, only what an author wrote down. So the harness asks: is the heuristic's ranking distinguishable from a random ranking of the declared labels? and answers with a permutation test.

Six controls, each against a different way of fooling oneself: a permutation test rather than a bare ρ; Bonferroni over the four testable signals (input_length clears α at p = 0.043 and dies at p = 0.174 corrected); a within-family control; η² on both sides, so a family effect cannot masquerade as a difficulty effect; a marginal-matched bucketing, so a bad marginal is not mistaken for a bad ranking; and a sweep of the whole weight simplex, so the null is a property of the signals rather than of one hand-chosen vector.

tool_use has zero variance here: a weighted signal with zero variance adds a constant, so it changes no ranking and no bucket. It is reported inert rather than credited, and a test proves that zeroing its weight leaves every bucket in place.

The harness can say yes

A calibration harness that only ever answers "no" proves nothing. A synthetic corpus of 60 examples whose prompt length really does track declared difficulty comes back ρ > 0.9, p < 0.01, trustworthy = True; shuffle its labels and it does not. Both are tests.

Equal scores always get equal buckets — every cut is score < cut, so no boundary splits a tie group, and the corpus has two tie groups of four. The price is that quantile cannot hit equal frequencies, so the report states the sizes it achieved. fixed leaves hard empty because the maximum score is 0.658: an absolute cut point on a log-compressed scale is unreachable for short-answer data. A warning fires, and that is why quantile is the default.

What the verdict changes

DifficultyReport.trustworthy is a single bit — true only when the correlation clears α, the sample clears 30 labelled examples, and the marginal-matched bucketing beats the majority baseline. Here it is False, and that forbids exactly one thing: the heuristic bucket must not be used as a difficulty label. evalforge difficulty --fail-on-uncalibrated exits 1.

The one downstream consumer that could have been fooled is difficulty stratification in the split builder. Milestone 7 chose the declared tags, arguing that a heuristic must not define the test set it is later evaluated on. This milestone turns that argument into a measurement — and finds a second, stronger reason: stratifying on the heuristic bucket would have stratified on task_family a second time, under a different name.

Nothing is deleted or imputed. Every example still gets a bucket, because the release builder and the dataset card must show a reader exactly what the audit saw.

A defect inherited from the label audit

An absent expected_output scores zero on output_complexity — the floor of the signal — so the examples whose labels most need attention sink to the easy end of the ranking.

All are examples the label audit flags. Milestone 6's MISSING defect corrupts Milestone 8's score; the report warns rather than imputing a value.

Limitations. The score is a surface proxy and is fooled here systematically, not occasionally. The weights are hand-chosen, not learned — fitting them to the 51 available labels would be fitting to the test set. The calibration target is one author's unaudited opinion: three hard examples out of 56, five with no declaration. A null result is consistent with "the heuristic is wrong" and with "the tags are noisy", and this corpus cannot separate the two. IRT-style difficulty from observed pass rates needs response data the offline default does not have.

Status

Complete. 163 tests, all CPU-only and offline. The score, the three bucketings, the distribution reports, the calibration harness, and the --fail-on-uncalibrated gate are implemented; the verdict on the committed fixtures is not calibrated, by measurement.

Open questions

  • Should the bucket ship at all when trustworthy is False? Emitting nothing would hide the evidence for the warning; emitting it risks a skimming reader.
  • The null is not attributable — heuristic or labels? A second annotator over 56 examples would settle it.
  • Should tool_use (inert here) and evidence_items (a family indicator here) stay in the default weights? Dropping them would flatter these fixtures and might be wrong for a corpus that populates that metadata.
